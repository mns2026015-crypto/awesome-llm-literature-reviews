## Overview

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly being used to assist with scientific literature reviews. They can retrieve information, summarize research findings, organize literature into themes, and generate structured reviews. However, rapidly evolving research fields such as artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and biomedicine create an important challenge: the knowledge available inside an LLM is limited by the temporal boundary of its training data.

A knowledge cutoff can cause an LLM to miss important research published after its training period. The problem is not limited to simply missing recent papers. The research literature examined in this repository highlights several additional problems, including differences between reported and effective knowledge cutoffs, temporal leakage during historical reasoning, temporal biases, citation hallucination, and evidence-decision decoupling.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), agentic literature synthesis, graph-guided retrieval, temporal verification, and sequentially ordered pre-training have been proposed to address these limitations. Systems such as OpenScholar, PaperQA2, IterSurvey, StructSurvey, Temporal Critique Fine-Tuning (TCFT), and Kairos represent different approaches to improving literature synthesis and temporal grounding.

The goal of this repository is to organize research and resources that help understand these challenges and the methods being developed to make automated literature reviews more accurate, evidence-grounded, and temporally robust.

## AI-Assisted Research Paper

### Knowledge Cutoff Effects on LLM-Generated Literature Reviews in Rapidly Evolving Fields

This research paper investigates how knowledge cutoffs influence the generation of automated literature reviews by Large Language Models. It examines the mechanisms and failure modes associated with temporal restrictions and discusses approaches for improving the temporal reliability of LLM-based scientific synthesis.

The paper discusses reported versus effective cutoffs, temporal leakage, temporal biases, citation hallucination, evidence-decision decoupling, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), agentic literature synthesis, graph-based retrieval, Temporal Critique Fine-Tuning (TCFT), and sequential snapshot pre-training.
